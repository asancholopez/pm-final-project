# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** Experienced drivers on long routes using Android phones.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** Finish every stop quickly and leave a delivery record they don't have to double-check. Ex: Log in the system the activity with the corresponding info required to complete their job
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** Core actions are slow and unreliable
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** WhatsApp/SMS messages to the dispatcher, a paper manifest, and phone calls to the office.
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** Before the shift, copy the stop list onto a paper manifest in case the app fails.
At each stop, skip the three-screen "Mark delivered" flow and text the dispatcher instead.
Retake the proof-of-delivery photo up to three times, because uploads fail silently on weak signal.
When the app crashes past ~40 stops, call the office and have the remaining stops read aloud (~20 min).
Finish the route from paper and messages, outside the app.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** The mid-route crash. Around stop 40, the app crashes and the rest of the route disappears. The driver has to stop, phone the office, and spend about 20 minutes rebuilding the route by voice, then finish the day off-app. It hits the most experienced drivers hardest, because they run the longest routes.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** 5 of 7 focus-group drivers keep a paper manifest specifically in case the app fails. A dispatcher called the WhatsApp group "the real system." "Mark delivered" is the top frontline complaint and has directly pushed drivers to text dispatchers instead.
